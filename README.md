# Cambio Card Game Rules

Cambio — also known as Cabo, Pablo, or Cactus — is a card game for 2+ players using a standard 54-card deck (52 cards plus 2 jokers). The goal: end the round with the fewest points. Rounds play fast, usually 10–20 minutes.

Source: [cambiocardgame.com](https://cambiocardgame.com/) (adapted for online play)

## Players

Cambio is played with 2 or more players. It plays best with 3–6. You need one standard 54-card deck (52 cards plus 2 jokers).

## Objective

The goal is to end the round with the lowest total point value in your 4-card hand. The round ends when one player calls "Cambio" — then everyone counts points and the lowest score wins.

Each player is given one chance before the first player picks a card from the deck to view 2 of their own cards and memorize them. After that you may not peek again unless you pull a 7 or 8.

## Setup

1. Shuffle a standard 54-card deck (52 cards plus 2 jokers).
2. Deal 4 cards face down to each player in a 2×2 grid.
3. Place the remaining deck face down in the center, with room for a discard pile.
4. Before play, each player may peek at 2 of their own cards — once. After that, no looking unless a special card lets you.

## Your Turn

1. Draw the top card from the deck **or** the discard pile.
2. **From the deck:** swap it with one of your face-down cards (old one goes face-up on the discard pile), OR discard it directly. If you discard it and it has a special ability, the ability triggers now.
3. **From the discard pile:** you must take the card — swap it with one of your face-down cards (old one goes face-up on the discard pile). You cannot place the drawn card back on the discard pile, and special abilities do not apply.
4. Play passes to the next player on the left.

**Scenario:** If you draw a 7 from the deck and want to swap it with your face-down 8, you're refusing to play the card — and since you can only play the cards you draw, the 8 you swapped and threw down does not count as a "Peek."

## Point Values

Number cards (2–10) are always face value. The host can configure point values for aces, jacks/queens, jokers, and black/red kings from the lobby before play starts (defaults below). Values are locked once a round begins and can be changed again after a round ends.

| Card | Default points |
| --- | --- |
| 2 – 10 | Face value |
| Jack | 10 |
| Queen | 10 |
| King ♣ ♠ (black) | −2 |
| King ♥ ♦ (red) | +25 |
| Ace | 1 |
| Joker | 0 |

Want the lowest score? Hold Aces, Jokers, and Black Kings. Get rid of Red Kings, face cards, and high numbers whenever you can.

## Special Cards

When you discard one of these after drawing from the deck, its ability activates. Swap it into your hand instead and nothing triggers. Cards drawn from the discard pile never trigger abilities.

| Card | Ability |
| --- | --- |
| 7 or 8 | **Peek** — Look at one of your own face-down cards in secret. |
| 9 or 10 | **Spy** — Look at one of another player's face-down cards in secret. |
| Jack | **Blind switch** — Swap one of your cards with another player's — without looking first. |
| Queen | **Look & switch** — Look at one card anywhere on the table, then swap one of your cards with another player's. |
| Any King | **Look & switch** — Look at 2 cards anywhere on the table, then swap one of your cards with another player's. |

**Cambio caller protection:** After a player calls Cambio, their cards cannot be targeted by Spy or any swap ability. The caller also cannot snap.

## Penalties

You can receive an extra penalty card if you make a wrong snap.

The extra penalty card is placed face down in your hand — you are not shown its value.

## Snapping

Snapping is one of the most powerful moves in Cambio — and the one most beginners misunderstand. If a card in your hand matches the top of the discard pile, you may snap it at any time, even on another player's turn. First to snap wins. A correct snap lets you discard one more card; a wrong snap costs you a penalty.

If a card is placed on the discard pile and a player knows one of their cards is the same, they can snap it on top of the matching card so they have one less card. Only one player may snap per discarded card — once someone has snapped, no other player can snap that card. Any player may snap even if it's not their turn.

If a player knows another player's card and snaps it, the player who snapped can give one of their own cards to the other player. If you snap a card and are wrong, you keep the card and receive a penalty card. If you attempt to snap another player's card and it's wrong, they keep the card and you still receive the penalty.

A player who has called Cambio cannot snap.

## Winning

Call "Cambio" at the start of your turn — before drawing from the deck or discard pile. Every other player gets one final turn, then all hands are revealed and the lowest total wins.

In order to win, one player has to call "Cambio" during their turn and cannot play a card if they call it. This must be done prior to the next player drawing a card. Once a player calls Cambio, the rest of the players get one more turn before everyone flips their cards and adds up their points. The person with the least amount of points wins.

After calling Cambio, the caller's cards are protected — other players cannot spy on or swap with the caller's cards, and the caller cannot snap.

If there is a tie, the player who didn't call Cambio wins. If there's a tie between two players who didn't call Cambio, the player with the least-value cards wins (e.g. Joker, Ace beats Black King, 2).

## Hints & Strategies

- The goal is simple: reduce your points as fast as possible, while knowing where your points are.
- If you get a blind switch (Jack), you can swap with someone's cards without looking. The advantage: you may know what card you're giving them, which lets you snap it later — and you play defense by giving them an unknown.
- Queens let you look at any card on the table before swapping — use that information to target high-value cards or set up future snaps.
- Kings let you look at 2 cards before swapping — powerful for finding Red Kings or high cards to offload.
- If you draw a card from the deck and discard it, you can immediately snap it after placing it on the discard pile — but only if no one else snaps first.
- Drawing from the discard pile is a safe way to swap without triggering abilities — useful when you want a known card without giving opponents a Spy or swap opportunity.

## FAQ

### How many players can play Cambio?

Cambio is played with 2 or more players. It works best with 3–6 people. You need one standard 54-card deck (52 cards plus 2 jokers). A typical round takes 10–20 minutes.

### How do you win Cambio?

To win, call "Cambio" at the start of your turn before drawing a card. Every other player then takes one final turn. Once all hands are revealed, the player with the lowest total point count wins the round.

### Can you draw from the discard pile?

Yes. On your turn you may draw from the deck or the discard pile. If you draw from the discard pile, you must swap the card into your hand — you cannot place it back on the discard pile. Special abilities do not apply to cards drawn from the discard pile.

### What does "snapping" a card mean in Cambio?

Snapping means placing a card from your hand face-up onto the discard pile because it matches a card already there — at any time, even during another player's turn. A correct snap lets you discard one additional card; a wrong snap adds a card to your hand as a penalty. Only one player may snap per discarded card.

### What's the difference between Cambio and Cabo?

Cambio and Cabo are essentially the same game — "Cambio" is one regional name, "Cabo" another. The core mechanics are identical: 4 cards per player, lowest score wins, special card abilities, and calling the game-ending word on your turn. It's also called Pablo or Cactus. This version includes several rule differences for online play.

### Can you play Cambio with a normal deck of cards?

Yes. Cambio uses a standard 54-card deck (52 cards plus 2 jokers). Jokers are worth 0 points and Black Kings are worth −2 — the most valuable cards to hold. Red Kings are worth +25 — the worst card to hold.

### How many cards do you start with in Cambio?

Each player starts with 4 cards, dealt face down in a 2×2 grid. Before the first turn, each player may peek at 2 of their own cards — once. After that you cannot look at your own cards again unless a 7 or 8 lets you.

### What are the special cards in Cambio?

Seven card types have abilities when discarded after drawing from the deck: 7 or 8 (Peek — your own card), 9 or 10 (Spy — an opponent's card), Jack (Blind switch — swap without looking), Queen (Look at one card anywhere, then swap), and any King (Look at 2 cards anywhere, then swap). Abilities never apply to cards drawn from the discard pile.

### What happens when someone calls "Cambio"?

The round enters its final phase. The caller cannot draw or play a card. Every other player takes one last turn. The caller's cards cannot be spied on or swapped, and the caller cannot snap. Then all cards are flipped, points counted, and the lowest total wins.

### What are the point values in Cambio?

Number cards (2–10) are face value. By default, aces are 1, jacks and queens are 10, black kings are −2, red kings are +25, and jokers are 0. The host can customize these five categories in the lobby before play starts; values are locked during an active round. The goal is the lowest total, so black kings, jokers, and aces are the most valuable to keep.

### What are the penalties in Cambio?

A wrong snap — snapping a card that doesn't match the top of the discard pile. You take the card back and receive an extra penalty card.
